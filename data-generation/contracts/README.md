# Opta-style event contract (v3)

> **In short:** the canonical events in every recording: Opta-style types and qualifiers derived from GRF's native contacts, decisions and tracking, plus an F24-style export. The feed sends them as `MatchEvent` v2 and MA3.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Status** | Implemented in the generator; consumed by the feed, ETL and backend |
| **Rules** | [`grf_demo/telemetry.py`](../grf_demo/telemetry.py), F24 IDs in [`grf_demo/opta.py`](../grf_demo/opta.py) |
| **Tests** | `tests/test_opta_events.py`, `tests/test_telemetry_v2.py` |

A real-time 90-minute GRF match produces one canonical recording and an Opta F24-style feed. Everything is synthetic: generated by a simulator and labelled `synthetic: true`. It is not Stats Perform data.

```text
GRF native contacts + decisions + 100 Hz tracking
        → grf_demo/telemetry.py  (documented derivation rules)
        → recording.json           canonical events consumed by ETL and the backend
        → recording.opta-f24.json  F24-style type/qualifier IDs (grf_demo/opta.py)
```

## Clock and location

- **Broadcast clock.** It runs through dead play, so each half ends in stoppage time. The second half starts at 45:00. First-half stoppage keeps `periodId` 1 with `timeMin` ≥ 45, as Opta does. **Order events by `sequence`, or by `(periodId, timeMin, timeSec)`; never by clock seconds alone.**
- `videoSeconds` equals capture seconds (real time) and never decreases.
- `x`, `y` are Opta 0–100 coordinates for the acting team: its attack runs towards `x=100`, and `y=0` is its right touchline. Canonical values can overshoot a line by about one unit where tracking does; the F24 export clamps to 0–100. Paired events (out, corner awarded) therefore mirror each other. Events with no team, such as period boundaries, have `null` coordinates.
- `possessionId` increases whenever the other team gains the ball, and at every taken restart; a restart event carries its new possession.

## Events

Events are timestamped when they **resolve**, so live delivery never reveals the future. A pass resolves at its first contact or stoppage. A shot resolves when it crosses a line, is caught, settles after a contact, or play stops.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `pass` | Intentional short/long/high pass. Success = next intentional contact by a different teammate. Qualifiers: `endX`, `endY`, `distanceMetres`, `angle`, `longBall` (> 32 m), `cross`, `chipped`, `switchOfPlay`, `offsidePass` | 1 / 2 |
| `clearance` | Target-less long, high or headed ball from x ≤ 25 with an opponent within 8 m | 12 |
| `shot` | Attempt; `outcome` is `off_target`, `post`, `saved` or `blocked`. Qualifiers: `situation`, `assistEventId`, `bigChance`, `goalMouthY`, `goalMouthZ` | 13 / 14 / 15 |
| `goal` | Score change. It inherits the attempt's location and qualifiers, and is never also a `shot`. When the conceding team touched it last, it is that player's `ownGoal` (no attempt qualifiers) | 16 (Q28) |
| `save` | Keeper contact (`saveType` caught/parried), or an outfield block (`defensiveBlock`). Only recorded once the ball has stayed out | 10 |
| `interception` | Opponent's first intentional contact on a pass moving ≥ 8 m/s before the contact, with no team-mate of the passer within 3 m, that is not a clearance and not a `blocked_pass` | 8 |
| `blocked_pass` | That contact within 4 m and 0.4 s of the pass | 74 |
| `tackle`, `dispossessed` | Interfere/sliding contact on an owner who has held the ball ≥ 0.4 s, settled by the next intentional contact: the tackler's team (or a stoppage without a foul) = 1; the owner's team-mate = 0; the owner himself = no tackle. If nobody plays it within 2 s, native ownership decides (it flickers to the tackler for a frame on contact, which is ignored). A whistled foul cancels it | 7, 50 |
| `take_on`, `challenge` | Owner gets 0.8 m past a goal-side opponent who was within 2 m. A failed take-on is only recorded when that opponent tackles him, so success ratios are not meaningful | 3, 45 |
| `aerial` | Header above 1 m with an opponent within 2 m; won/lost pair | 44 |
| `ball_recovery` | Gaining a loose or dying ball, confirmed by the same team's next contact | 49 |
| `out`, `corner_awarded` | Ball over a line; the team that put it out (`failure`, with player) is paired with the other team (`success`) | 5, 6 |
| `keeper_claim`, `keeper_pickup`, `punch` | Retention from a ball above 1.2 m (claim), lower (pick-up); high deflection without retention (punch) | 11, 52, 41 |
| `offside`, `offside_provoked` | Referee decision; the deepest outfield defender provokes it. The export sends one offside pass (type 2) per call, by the offside team: the flagged pass, or else that team's last touch, with the call's id and time | –, 55 |
| `error` | Turnover in the own defensive third followed within 6 s by the opponent's attempt (`leadsTo` shot/goal) | 51 |
| `ball_touch` | Accidental contact by the team in possession that gives the ball to the opponent | 61 |
| `foul`, `foul_won`, `card` | Whistled native decisions; card colour and reason. `penaltyAwarded` only when the next taken restart is a penalty kick (the referee's decision, not the foul's location) | 4, 17 |
| `corner`, `free_kick`, `throw_in`, `goal_kick`, `kick_off`, `penalty` | Taken restarts. In F24 these are qualifiers on the taker's delivery within 10 s; a restart whose taker dribbles on and never delivers has no F24 row | Q6, Q5, Q107, Q124, Q279, Q9 |
| `touch` | Every executed contact (not an F24 event) | – |
| `period_start`, `period_end` | Phase transitions | 32, 30 |

The F24 export adds team set-up (34: 4-3-3, positions, shirt numbers), per-team `event_id` counters, `Q55` related-event links, `Q210` on passes leading to attempts, and zones (`Q56`). IDs follow the public F24 appendices as mirrored by [kloppy](https://github.com/PySport/kloppy) and [socceraction](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/socceraction).

## Not available

Substitutions, injuries, VAR and xG are not available, and shirt numbers do not change. Absent evidence stays unknown rather than zero. Tackle, take-on, aerial and recovery thresholds are rules over simulated tracking, not human annotation.

Simulated football turns the ball over far more often than top-flight football, and the adapter reports what happened rather than rescaling it. Expect per-match counts of ball recoveries, clearances and passes well above Opta norms (several hundred recoveries against about 100; about 1,300–1,500 passes at 75–78% against about 950 at about 83%). GRF also restarts less often, with roughly a third of a real match's throw-ins and corners, and rarely blocks shots. The [recordings page](../docs/components/recordings.md) gives each shipped match's score; `tests/test_fixture_recordings.py` pins two of them (`riverton-forgeham` is pinned in `tests/test_recording.py`).

## Realism patch

`grf_demo/prepare_realism.py` adds runtime knobs to a disposable engine copy. Unset knobs keep upstream behaviour. `scenario.REALISM` holds the tuned values, which are recorded in `provenance.realism`. Scripted tactics create more chances, so the shot threshold rose from 1.31 to 1.33 (Redcastle Town–Port Albion was rendered at 1.32).

Upstream GRF scores 15–60 goals per 90 minutes: keepers rush the shooter, auto-aim finds the inside of the post, every target-less clearance can become a shot, passes ignore the offside line and contact fouls are never whistled. Native AIs occasionally freeze around a stationary ball owner. After 8 s of live play without a contact, the capture presses and releases long pass for the owner's team (the native controller passes on release), then hands control back to the built-in AI. `provenance.stall_nudge_seconds` lists when each nudge happened, so any pass, shot or goal that follows one is traceable. A press cannot move a goalkeeper (never auto-selected), so a keeper-owned freeze would run on; the capture aborts if 60 s pass without a contact. Headless validation over full matches and the shipped render are summarised on the recordings page.
